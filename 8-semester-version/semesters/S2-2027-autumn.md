## PHY 102: Electricity & Magnetism I

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.02/8.022, Harvard Physics 15b, Oxford CP2, Dhaka PH 103.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 101, MTH 101 (C or better). MTH 102 taken concurrently.
**Description:** Electrostatics, potential, conductors, currents and circuits, the fields of moving charges (where magnetism emerges from relativity, which is Purcell's signature approach), magnetostatics, induction, AC circuits, Maxwell's equations, electromagnetic waves, and fields in matter.

**Primary:** Purcell & Morin, *Electricity and Magnetism* (3rd ed.), "P&M" below.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.02 (Lewin / TEAL). MITx 8.02x for practice.
**Supplements:** Feynman Vol. II; Tong, *Electromagnetism*; Halliday-Resnick-Walker for an easier pass.

| 6–7 | Conductors, capacitance, energy in the field, the method of images (intro) | P&M 3 | PS4 |
| 8 | Currents, Ohm's law, DC circuits, RC circuits | P&M 4 | PS5 |
| 9–10 | Fields of moving charges; magnetism as a relativistic effect | P&M 5 | PS6 |
| 11 | **Midterm**; light week | – | Midterm |
| 12 | Magnetic field: Biot–Savart, Ampère, vector potential | P&M 6 | PS7 |

## MTH 102: Multivariable & Vector Calculus

**Modelled on:** MIT 18.02, Harvard Math 21a, Oxford CP4 (part), Caltech Ma 1bc, Dhaka Math-103.
**Prerequisites:** MTH 101. **ASE available.**
**Description:** Vectors and matrices, partial derivatives, gradients, optimisation with constraints, multiple integrals in curvilinear coordinates, and the vector calculus of line and surface integrals and the theorems of Green, Gauss and Stokes. This is the language of PHY 102 and PHY 205.

| 8 | Optimisation, the second-derivative test, Lagrange multipliers | PS5 |
| 9–10 | Double integrals; polar coordinates; the Jacobian | PS6 |
| 11 | **Midterm** | Midterm |

## MTH 104: Differential Equations

**Modelled on:** MIT 18.03, Oxford CP3/CP4 (ODEs), Caltech Ma 2 (part), Dhaka Math-203.
**Prerequisites:** MTH 102. MTH 103 concurrently. **ASE available.**
**Description:** First- and second-order linear ODEs, resonance, Fourier series, Laplace transforms, the delta function and Green's function, linear systems and the matrix exponential, nonlinear systems and the phase plane, and a first look at the heat and wave PDEs solved by separation of variables.

**Primary:** MIT 18.03 notes (Miller, Orloff) with Boas ch. 7–8 and 13.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 18.03SC Differential Equations.
**Supplements:** Tenenbaum & Pollard; Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* ch. 5–6.

| Week | Topic | Assessed |

| 3 | Numerical methods and existence/uniqueness (links to CMP 101) | PS2 |
| 4–5 | Complex numbers and exponentials; second-order linear ODEs with constant coefficients | PS3 |
| 6–7 | Inhomogeneous equations: undetermined coefficients, resonance, frequency response | PS4 |
| 8 | Fourier series; solving ODEs with periodic forcing | PS5 |
| 9–10 | The delta function, impulse response, convolution, Green's functions | PS6 |
| 11 | **Midterm** | Midterm |
| 12 | Laplace transforms | PS7 |
| 13–14 | Linear systems: eigenvalue methods, the matrix exponential | PS8 |
| 15 | Phase portraits, stability, linearisation | PS9 |
| 16 | Nonlinear systems: pendulum, Lotka–Volterra, limit cycles | PS10 |
| 17–18 | PDEs: the heat equation by separation of variables | PS11 |
| 19 | The wave equation; d'Alembert's solution; links to PHY 103 | Quiz |
| 20–21 | Revision and **final exam** | Final |

**Assessment:** problem sets 25% · quizzes 5% · tutorials 10% · midterm 20% · final 40%.

## LAB 101: Experimental Physics I, Measurement & Uncertainty

**Modelled on:** Harvard 15a/b PSI labs, MIT 8.01/8.02 TEAL labs, Oxford first-year practicals, Caltech Ph 3, Dhaka PL 101.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 101, CMP 101.
**Description:** How to measure, estimate and report uncertainty; propagate errors; fit models with χ²; keep a lab notebook; and write a report in journal style. The experiments are done at home with a smartphone (phyphox), an Arduino and simple apparatus, and every analysis is done in Python.

**Primary:** Hughes & Hase, *Measurements and their Uncertainties* (Oxford).
**Supplements:** Taylor, *An Introduction to Error Analysis*; the phyphox experiment documentation.

| Weeks | Experiment / topic | Report? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lab safety, the notebook, significant figures, types of error, histograms | – |
| 2–3 | **E1 Pendulum and g**: period vs length and amplitude with phyphox; finite-amplitude correction; χ² fit | **Report 1** |
| 4 | Error propagation, weighted means, the Gaussian and Poisson distributions | Pre-lab quiz |
| 5–6 | **E2 Damped oscillator**: spring-mass with the phone accelerometer; measure γ and Q; fit an exponential envelope | **Report 2** |
| 7 | **Oral presentation 1**: E1 or E2 (10 min plus questions) | Oral |
| 8–9 | **E3 RC circuit and the Arduino ADC**: charging and discharging, time constant, calibration of the ADC, systematic errors | **Report 3** |
| 10 | Least squares in depth: residuals, correlated parameters, the goodness of fit | Pre-lab quiz |
| 11–12 | **E4 Magnetic field of a coil or of a long wire**: phone magnetometer vs Biot–Savart; distance dependence; Earth's field as a background | **Report 4** |
| 13 | **E5 Mini-experiment of your own design** (for example, the speed of sound or Newton's law of cooling); notebook only | Notebook |
| 14 | **Oral presentation 2** | Oral |
| 15 | **Final practical**: analyse an unseen dataset under time pressure (2 h) | Final |

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · reports 45% · oral presentations 15% · pre-lab quizzes 10% · final practical 15%.

**Safety:** use a Class 2 laser pointer only and never look into the beam. Use only low-voltage (≤ 12 V DC) circuits, and never work on mains electricity.

## PHY 203: Special Relativity

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.033, Oxford CP1 (SR part) and B2 (part), Harvard 15a (part), Dhaka PH 301 (part).
**Prerequisites:** PHY 102, MTH 103.
**Primary:** Morin, *Special Relativity for the Enthusiastic Beginner*, then Taylor & Wheeler, *Spacetime Physics*.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.033 Relativity and 8.20 Introduction to Special Relativity.
**Supplements:** French, *Special Relativity*; Steane, *Relativity Made Relatively Easy*.

1. Galilean relativity, the Michelson–Morley experiment, Einstein's postulates
2. Simultaneity, time dilation, length contraction, clock synchronisation
3. The Lorentz transformation, velocity addition, the relativistic Doppler effect
4. Spacetime diagrams, invariant intervals, causality, paradoxes (twin, pole-in-barn)
5. Four-vectors, proper time, four-velocity and four-momentum
6. Energy and momentum, E = mc², massless particles
7. **Midterm**
8. Collisions, decays, threshold energies, the centre-of-momentum frame
9. Relativistic forces, acceleration, hyperbolic motion, rapidity
10. Relativity and electromagnetism: transformation of E and B, the field tensor (preview of PHY 205)
11. The equivalence principle, accelerated frames, gravitational redshift
12. The Schwarzschild metric (intro), geodesics, light deflection, Shapiro delay, GPS
13. Newtonian cosmology (intro, following MIT 8.033)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## EXM 100: Prelims (Annual Examination I)

**Week 22 (13–19 Dec 2027).** The format follows the Oxford Prelims (CP1–CP4) and the Dhaka viva PV 102. In this version Year 1 contains special relativity (as the Oxford Prelims do) and waves move to Year 2.

| Paper | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Physics | PHY 101, PHY 102, PHY 203. Section A has 10 short questions, all compulsory. Section B has 3 long questions chosen from 6 | 3 h |
| Paper 2: Mathematical Methods | MTH 101–104. Same format | 3 h |
| Viva voce | 30 min oral covering the year's physics and your own lab and computing work | 30 min |

Weighting: papers 80%, viva 20%. You need ≥ 50% to progress to Year 2. See [assessment-and-grading.md §7](../../docs/assessment-and-grading.md).

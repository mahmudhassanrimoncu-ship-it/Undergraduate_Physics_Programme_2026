## PHY 206: Quantum Physics II

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.05, Harvard Physics 143a/b, Oxford A3/B3 parts, Princeton PHY 305, Caltech Ph 125a, Dhaka PH 402.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 201, MTH 103.
**Primary:** Zwiebach, *Mastering Quantum Mechanics*.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.05 Quantum Physics II (Zwiebach).
**Supplements:** Shankar; Binney & Skinner (free, Oxford); Townsend.

1. Two-state systems, the Stern–Gerlach experiment, spin as a vector space
2. Vector spaces, linear operators, inner products, adjoints (as in MIT 8.05)
3. Dirac notation, the spectral theorem, complete sets of commuting observables
4. The uncertainty principle (rigorous version, plus the energy–time relation)
5. The Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures; time evolution
6. The harmonic oscillator algebraically; coherent and squeezed states
7. **Midterm**
8. Two-state dynamics: spin precession, NMR, Rabi oscillations
9. Multiparticle states, tensor products, entanglement
10. EPR, Bell inequalities, teleportation
11. Angular momentum algebra; central potentials revisited
12. Addition of angular momentum, Clebsch–Gordan coefficients
13. Density matrices, mixed states, decoherence (intro)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## LAB 301: Advanced Laboratory I (Junior Lab)

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.13 (Experimental Physics I), Harvard Physics 191, Caltech Ph 77, Princeton PHY 312, Dhaka PL 301.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 201, LAB 101, CMP 201.
**Primary:** MIT OCW 8.13/8.14 Junior Lab manuals and the original papers each one cites.
**Supplements:** Bevington & Robinson.

As in MIT 8.13, you carry out **four experiments** in modern physics. Two are real home experiments and two analyse **real research data**. Each one ends in a journal-style report. Two of them also get a 20-minute MIT-style oral presentation with questions.

| Weeks | Experiment | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **J1 Planck's constant from LEDs**: turn-on voltage vs wavelength for 4–6 LEDs, wavelengths measured with your own grating spectrometer | Home |
| 4–6 | **J2 Atomic spectroscopy**: build a grating spectrometer; measure mercury and sodium lines (fluorescent tube, street lamp); calibrate; compare with NIST; the Rydberg constant from hydrogen data | Home + NIST data |
| 7 | **Oral presentation 1** | – |
| 8–10 | **J3 Particle physics from CMS open data**: reconstruct the dimuon invariant-mass spectrum; find the J/ψ, Υ and Z; fit the Z mass and width | CERN Open Data |
| 11–13 | **J4 Gravitational waves from LIGO open data**: whitening, band-passing, matched filtering of GW150914; estimate the chirp mass | GWOSC |
| 14 | **Oral presentation 2** | – |
| 15 | **Final**: an unseen data-analysis practical (3 h) | – |

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · reports 45% · orals 15% · pre-lab quizzes 10% · final practical 15%.

## IND 301: Junior Paper I

**Modelled on:** Princeton Junior Papers (one in each semester of junior year), Harvard Physics 90r, Oxford's mini-project.
**Prerequisites:** Year 2 complete.
**Task:** Choose an approved topic. Read the primary literature, **reproduce a key published result** (an analytic derivation or a numerical calculation), and write it up as a ~5,000-word paper in *Physical Review* style using LaTeX.

**Example topics:** reproducing the Lorenz attractor and its Lyapunov exponent; Onsager's Ising solution checked numerically; the Bohr–Sommerfeld vs exact spectra of anharmonic oscillators; the Kronig–Penney band structure; the Chandrasekhar limit; the Rabi model; the quantum Zeno effect; the fluid dynamics of a draining tank.

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Topic discussion. Three candidate topics proposed |
| 3 | **Proposal** (1 page): the question, key references and the planned reproduction |
| 4–8 | Literature review; outline due in week 5 |
| 9–15 | Reproduction and analysis; weekly 20-minute progress meetings |
| 16 | Complete draft |
| 17–18 | Written feedback (the editor role) |
| 19–20 | Revision; final paper due at the end of week 14 |
| 21 | **20-minute oral presentation** plus questions |

**Assessment:** proposal 10% · progress meetings 15% · paper 55% · presentation 20%.

## PHY 401: General Relativity & Cosmology

**Modelled on:** Oxford B5, MIT 8.962/8.286/8.224, Harvard Physics 210, Caltech Ph 236, Dhaka PH 308 (part).
**Prerequisites:** PHY 203, PHY 204, MTH 201.
**Primary:** Hartle, *Gravity* (weeks 1–10), then Ryden, *Introduction to Cosmology* (weeks 11–13).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.962 General Relativity (Hughes) and 8.286 The Early Universe (Guth).
**Supplements:** Carroll, *Spacetime and Geometry*; Schutz; Tong, *General Relativity*.

1. The equivalence principle; gravity as geometry
2. Metrics, the line element, geodesics from the variational principle
3. The Schwarzschild geometry: orbits, perihelion precession
4. Light bending, gravitational lensing, the Shapiro delay, GPS
5. Black holes: horizons, Kruskal coordinates, a qualitative account of Hawking radiation
6. Gravitational waves in linearised gravity; LIGO (links to LAB 301 J4)
7. **Midterm**
8. Tensors and differential geometry: covariant derivatives, the Christoffel symbols
9. Curvature: the Riemann, Ricci and Einstein tensors
10. The Einstein field equations; the stress-energy tensor; the Newtonian limit
11. FRW cosmology, the Friedmann equations, cosmic expansion, the Hubble constant
12. Thermal history: nucleosynthesis, recombination, the CMB
13. Inflation, dark matter, dark energy, open problems
14. Revision
15. **Final**

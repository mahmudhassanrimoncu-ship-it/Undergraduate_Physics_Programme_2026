## PHY 103: Vibrations, Waves & Optics

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.03, Harvard Physics 15c, Oxford CP4 (waves) and A2 (optics), Dhaka PH 201 and PH 205.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 102, MTH 102. MTH 103 and MTH 104 concurrently.
**Description:** Oscillators, coupled oscillators and normal modes; continuous systems and the wave equation; Fourier analysis; dispersion, wave packets and group velocity; reflection and transmission; electromagnetic waves and polarisation; geometric optics; interference and diffraction. This course is the bridge to quantum mechanics.

**Primary:** Georgi, *The Physics of Waves* (free, Harvard 15c) and Hecht, *Optics* (chapters 4–10).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.03SC Physics III: Vibrations and Waves.
**Supplements:** French, *Vibrations and Waves*; Morin's waves notes; Crawford, *Waves*.

## PHY 202: Thermal & Statistical Physics I

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.044, Oxford A1, Princeton PHY 301, Caltech Ph 12c, Dhaka PH 102.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 101, MTH 102. PHY 201 concurrently, as MIT recommends for 8.044.
**Primary:** Schroeder, *An Introduction to Thermal Physics*, ch. 1–7.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.044 notes and problem sets.
**Supplements:** Blundell & Blundell, *Concepts in Thermal Physics*; Kittel & Kroemer.

1. Probability: random variables, distributions, the central limit theorem (8.044 opens here)
2. Temperature, ideal gases, equipartition, heat and work, the first law
3. Heat capacities, enthalpy; kinetic theory and the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
4. The second law: multiplicity, Einstein solids, entropy
5. Interactions: temperature, pressure and chemical potential from entropy
6. Engines and refrigerators, the Carnot cycle, real cycles (Otto, Diesel, as in DU PH 102)
7. **Midterm**
8. Free energies, Maxwell relations, thermodynamic identities
9. Phase transformations, the Clausius–Clapeyron relation, van der Waals
10. The Boltzmann factor, the partition function, the canonical ensemble
11. Paramagnetism, equipartition revisited, the Maxwell speed distribution
12. Quantum statistics: Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein distributions
13. Blackbody radiation, degenerate Fermi gases, Debye solids (preview)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 204: Classical Mechanics II

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.223 + 8.09, Harvard Physics 151, Princeton PHY 205, Caltech Ph 106a, Dhaka PH 301 and PH 205.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 101, MTH 104.
**Primary:** Taylor, *Classical Mechanics*, ch. 6–16 (selected).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.223 Classical Mechanics II and 8.09 Classical Mechanics III.
**Supplements:** Tong, *Classical Dynamics*; Hand & Finch; Goldstein; Landau & Lifshitz Vol. 1 (honours).

1. The calculus of variations, the Euler–Lagrange equation, brachistochrone, geodesics
2. Lagrangian mechanics, generalised coordinates, constraints
3. Lagrange multipliers and constraint forces; symmetries and Noether's theorem
4. The two-body problem and central forces from the Lagrangian; scattering and the Rutherford cross-section
5. Rigid bodies: the inertia tensor, principal axes, Euler's equations, the free symmetric top
6. Small oscillations: the normal-mode matrix method
7. **Midterm**
8. The Hamiltonian, Hamilton's equations, phase space, Liouville's theorem
9. Poisson brackets, canonical transformations (with the link to quantum commutators)
10. Hamilton–Jacobi theory, action-angle variables, adiabatic invariants
11. Nonlinear dynamics and chaos: the driven damped pendulum, Poincaré sections (done numerically, links to CMP 101)
12. Continuous systems: Lagrangian density for the string, field equations
13. The relativistic Lagrangian, a charged particle in an EM field, the action for Maxwell's equations (following MIT 8.223)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## MTH 201: Mathematical Methods

**Modelled on:** MIT 18.04/18.075, Harvard AM 104, Oxford Part A Mathematical Methods, Caltech ACM 95ab, Dhaka PH 203.
**Prerequisites:** MTH 102, MTH 104.
**Primary:** Boas, *Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences* (ch. 10–15).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 18.04 Complex Variables with Applications.
**Supplements:** Riley, Hobson & Bence; Needham, *Visual Complex Analysis*; Arfken (reference).

1. Complex functions, analyticity, the Cauchy–Riemann equations, harmonic functions
2. Contour integrals, Cauchy's theorem and integral formula
3. Laurent series, singularities, residues
4. Evaluating real integrals by residues; branch cuts
5. Conformal mapping (2D electrostatics and potential flow)
6. Fourier transforms in depth: convolution, Parseval, applications to diffusion and waves
7. **Midterm**
8. Sturm–Liouville theory, orthogonal functions, eigenfunction expansions
9. Legendre polynomials, associated Legendre functions, spherical harmonics
10. Bessel functions; Laplace's equation in cylindrical and spherical coordinates
11. Separation of variables: the heat, wave and Schrödinger equations in 2D and 3D
12. Green's functions for ODEs and PDEs, the method of images
13. Tensors: index notation, the summation convention, Cartesian tensors (preview for PHY 205 and PHY 401)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## LAB 201: Electronics for Physicists

**Modelled on:** Harvard Physics 113 / 123, Caltech APh/EE 23-24, Dhaka PH 202 and PH 403.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 102, LAB 101.
**Primary:** Hayes & Horowitz, *Learning the Art of Electronics* (Harvard's Physics 123 lab text).
**Supplements:** Horowitz & Hill, *The Art of Electronics* (3rd ed.); Boylestad & Nashelsky.
**Extra kit:** a low-cost USB or kit oscilloscope (for example a DSO138 or a USB scope), a function generator (an Arduino-based one is fine), op-amps (TL072, LM358), BJTs (2N3904/3906), diodes and Zeners. Use the Falstad or LTspice simulators alongside the bench.

1. DC circuits, voltage dividers, Thévenin and Norton equivalents, instrument loading
2. RC circuits in the frequency domain: filters, Bode plots, phasors
3. Diodes: rectifiers, clamps, Zener regulation, LEDs
4. BJT transistors: the switch, the follower, the common-emitter amplifier, biasing
5. FETs and MOSFET switches; current sources and mirrors
6. Op-amps I: golden rules, inverting and non-inverting amplifiers
7. **Midterm practical**: design and build, then measure against specification
8. Op-amps II: integrators, differentiators, active filters, non-idealities
9. Oscillators and comparators, Schmitt triggers, 555 timers
10. Digital logic: gates, flip-flops, counters (Dhaka PH 104 revisited at a physics level)
11. ADC/DAC, sampling, aliasing, microcontroller interfacing (Arduino)
12. Noise, grounding, shielding, and the lock-in amplifier concept
13. PID feedback control: a temperature- or position-control project
14. Project completion
15. **Final practical**: build and debug against a specification

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · build reports 45% · oral demonstrations 15% · pre-lab quizzes 10% · final practical 15%.

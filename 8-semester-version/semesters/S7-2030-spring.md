## PHY 303: Atomic, Molecular & Laser Physics

**Modelled on:** Oxford B3 (Atomic & laser physics), Harvard Physics 175, MIT 8.421 (introductory parts), Dhaka PH 204 and PH 306.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 301, PHY 205.
**Primary:** Foot, *Atomic Physics* (Oxford Master Series).
**Supplements:** Bransden & Joachain; Milonni & Eberly, *Laser Physics*; Saleh & Teich.

1. Hydrogen revisited: the Bohr model, gross structure and spectroscopy history
2. Fine structure, the Lamb shift (qualitative), hyperfine structure, the 21 cm line
3. Helium, the Pauli principle, exchange
4. Multi-electron atoms: central field, LS and jj coupling, Hund's rules, the periodic table
5. Atoms in fields: Zeeman (weak and strong field), Paschen–Back, Stark
6. Radiative transitions: Einstein A and B coefficients, selection rules, line broadening
7. **Midterm**
8. Lasers I: population inversion, gain, threshold, rate equations
9. Lasers II: optical cavities, Gaussian beams, longitudinal and transverse modes
10. Laser systems: HeNe, diode, Nd:YAG, Ti:sapphire; pulsed lasers and mode locking
11. Molecules: Born–Oppenheimer, rotational and vibrational spectra, Raman scattering
12. Laser cooling and trapping, optical tweezers, BEC experiments
13. Photonics: optical fibres, nonlinear optics (SHG), applications (Dhaka PH 306)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 304: Condensed Matter Physics

**Modelled on:** Oxford B6, MIT 8.231, Harvard Physics 195, Dhaka PH 303 and PH 405.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 301, PHY 302.
**Primary:** Simon, *The Oxford Solid State Basics*.
**Lectures:** Steven Simon's Oxford lectures; MIT OCW 8.231.
**Supplements:** Kittel, *Introduction to Solid State Physics*; Ashcroft & Mermin (reference); Tong, *Solid State Physics*.

1. Specific heat of solids: Einstein and Debye
2. Electrons in metals: the Drude and Sommerfeld models
3. Chemical bonding; the origin of crystal structure
4. 1D models: phonons, the monatomic and diatomic chain, dispersion
5. 1D tight-binding; the origin of bands
6. Crystal structure: lattices, the unit cell, Miller indices
7. **Midterm**
8. The reciprocal lattice, Brillouin zones, X-ray and neutron diffraction
9. The nearly free electron model, band gaps, Bloch's theorem
10. Insulators, metals and semiconductors; effective mass, holes
11. Semiconductor devices: doping, the p-n junction, transistors (links to LAB 201)
12. Magnetism: dia- and paramagnetism, ferromagnetism, the mean-field Weiss model, domains
13. A survey of superconductivity, topological matter and 2D materials (graphene)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 305: Nuclear & Particle Physics

**Modelled on:** Oxford B4, MIT 8.276/8.701, Harvard Physics 145, Dhaka PH 304 and PH 406.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 301, PHY 203.
**Primary:** Griffiths, *Introduction to Elementary Particles*, plus Krane, *Introductory Nuclear Physics* (selected).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.701 Introduction to Nuclear and Particle Physics.
**Supplements:** Martin & Shaw; Povh et al.

1. Nuclear properties: size, mass, binding energy, the semi-empirical mass formula
2. The nuclear force, the deuteron, isospin
3. The shell model, magic numbers, spin and parity
4. Radioactive decay: α (Gamow tunnelling), β (Fermi theory), γ
5. Fission, fusion, nucleosynthesis, reactors
6. Detectors and accelerators; how particle physics is measured (links to LAB 301 J3)
7. **Midterm**
8. Relativistic kinematics, cross-sections and decay rates, Fermi's golden rule
9. The particle zoo, the quark model, symmetries and conservation laws
10. Feynman diagrams; Griffiths' toy theory; QED processes
11. QCD: colour, confinement, asymptotic freedom, jets
12. Weak interactions, parity violation, CP violation, neutrino oscillations
13. Electroweak unification, the Higgs mechanism (qualitative), the Standard Model and beyond
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## LAB 401: Advanced Laboratory II, Experiment Design Project

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.14 (an exploratory project developed from proposal and design through to a poster session), Caltech Ph 77, Harvard Physics 191, Dhaka PH 404 and PL 401.
**Prerequisites:** LAB 301.

There is one short guided experiment, followed by a **self-designed experiment** that runs from proposal to poster.

| Weeks | Work |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | **Guided: cosmic-ray muons** with a CosmicWatch-style desktop muon detector (an MIT-designed open-hardware scintillator and SiPM detector, about US$100 in parts). Measure the rate vs angle (cos²θ) and vs elevation |
| 4 | **Project proposal**: physics question, apparatus, expected signal, uncertainty budget, risks |
| 5–6 | Design review and build |
| 7 | **Oral: design review** |
| 8–12 | Data taking, analysis, iteration |
| 13 | Poster and report draft |
| 14 | Final report (journal style) |
| 15 | **Poster session / oral defence** |

**Project ideas:** a Michelson interferometer measuring thermal expansion; a Kater reversible pendulum (g to 10⁻⁴); a chaotic double pendulum vs simulation; an optical-lever torsion balance; acoustic levitation and standing waves; a homemade magnetometer tracking geomagnetic variation; measuring Planck's constant from blackbody radiation; laser speckle statistics.

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · reports 45% (guided report 15%, project report 30%) · orals 15% · proposal and design review 10% · poster/defence 15%.

**Senior Thesis proposal (due at the end of week 13):** 3–4 pages giving the research question, background, method, timeline and risks. Approval is required before THS 401.

## THS 401: Senior Thesis I

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.THU, Princeton's senior thesis, and the Oxford MPhys project (about one term of full-time-equivalent work).
**Prerequisites:** IND 302, plus a thesis proposal approved at the end of S6 (due in week 19).

A thesis must contain **original work**. Realistic forms for an independent researcher are:

- **Computational or theoretical:** a numerical study of a model system, or an analytic extension of a published calculation.
- **Open-data analysis:** a new analysis of LIGO, CERN, SDSS, TESS/Kepler, Gaia or Planck data.
- **Experimental:** an extension of the LAB 401 project to research quality.

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Kick-off: refine the plan and set up the repository (`work/thesis/`) |
| 3–8 | Literature review (written chapter, about 3,000 words) |
| 9–10 | **Proposal and literature review** submitted (graded) |
| 11–18 | Research; weekly progress meetings (supervisor role) |
| 17–18 | **Progress report** (graded) |
| 21 | **Interim results presentation** (25 min plus questions) |

**Assessment:** proposal and literature review 25% · progress report 25% · interim presentation 25% · supervisor assessment of progress (effort, independence, rigour) 25%.

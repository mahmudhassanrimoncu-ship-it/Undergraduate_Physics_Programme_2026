# The 8-semester version (for comparison)

> **This is not the plan you are following.** It is the same physics degree laid out as **8 semesters (2 a year)** instead of 12 terms (3 a year), built so that you can compare the two yourself. The courses, syllabi, textbooks, learning outcomes, grading, annual exams, viva, junior papers and thesis are identical. What changes is the calendar, how many courses run at once, and the order of a few courses. Nothing in the main programme has changed. If you decide to switch, this folder becomes the plan.

**What moves, and why.** The physics core keeps MIT's order (8.01 → 8.02 → 8.03 → 8.04 → 8.05 → 8.06), one step per semester, so with fewer semesters some courses have to shift:

- **Special relativity (PHY 203) moves into Year 1**, as in the Oxford Prelims. **Waves (PHY 103) moves into Year 2**, as at MIT, where 8.03 is a sophomore course.
- **General relativity, quantum information and the nonlinear dynamics course move earlier.** **Atomic, condensed-matter and nuclear and particle physics (PHY 303–305) move into Year 4.** So the Finals get one extra paper on them (C4).
- **Four prerequisites are taken alongside instead of before:** MTH 103 with MTH 101, MTH 104 with MTH 102, PHY 203 with PHY 102, and PHY 306 with CMP 201. MIT and Oxford do the same with the first three.
- **One loss:** thermal physics (PHY 202) no longer runs alongside Quantum I (PHY 201), which MIT recommends for 8.044.
- **Elective I comes in Year 3**, so it must be one whose prerequisites are met by then (ELE 410, 413, 415, 416, 417 or 419).

## What to look for when you compare

**Arguments for the 8-semester version**
- **Fewer exam seasons and longer breaks.** You get 8 exam seasons instead of 12, a real winter break, and the annual exam lands in December instead of January.
- **More time for each subject to settle.** Each course runs over 22 weeks.
- **It may line up with your CSE semesters.** If your CSE year has two semesters with exams at fixed times, physics can be set to avoid them every year.
- **It finishes a month earlier** (22 Dec 2030).

**Arguments for the 12-term version**
- **Fewer subjects at once.** You hold 3 subjects in your head instead of 4–5, which is what the [teaching method](../docs/teaching-method.md) recommends for a learner studying about 20 hours a week.
- **More continuous practice.** There's a problem set in every course every week, so each topic is still fresh when you practise it.
- **Exams are spread out.** Each exam season has 3 papers, not 4–5.
- **About 4 more study weeks a year** (48 vs 44).

**Things to check against your own life**
- Put both calendars beside your CSE calendar for 2027 and see which one clashes less.
- Look at a 5-course semester (for example [S3](semesters/S3-2028-spring.md)) and ask whether five subjects at once feels manageable alongside CSE.
- Compare the two Semester 1 timetables. The weekly hours are the same; the difference is 3 courses at a time against 4.

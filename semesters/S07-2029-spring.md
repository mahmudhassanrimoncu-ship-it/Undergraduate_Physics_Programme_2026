## PHY 301: Quantum Physics III

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.06, Harvard Physics 143b, Oxford B3, Princeton PHY 305, Caltech Ph 125b, Dhaka PH 402.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 206.
**Primary:** Zwiebach, *Mastering Quantum Mechanics* (Part III), plus Griffiths & Schroeter Part II.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.06 Quantum Physics III (Zwiebach).
**Supplements:** Sakurai & Napolitano (honours); Tong, *Applications of Quantum Mechanics*.

1. Natural units and the scales of microscopic physics
2. Non-degenerate perturbation theory
3. Degenerate perturbation theory
4. The hydrogen atom: fine structure, spin–orbit coupling, relativistic corrections
5. The Zeeman and Stark effects; hyperfine structure
6. The variational method; the helium atom; Born–Oppenheimer
7. **Midterm**
8. The WKB approximation, tunnelling, alpha decay
9. A charged particle in a magnetic field: Landau levels, the integer quantum Hall effect
10. Time-dependent perturbation theory, Fermi's golden rule
11. Interaction with radiation: absorption, stimulated and spontaneous emission, selection rules
12. The adiabatic theorem, the Berry phase
13. Scattering: partial waves, phase shifts, the Born approximation, resonances
14. Revision
15. **Final**

**Term paper (as in MIT 8.06, CI-M):** a 4,000-word paper on a topic related to Quantum II and III, for example quantum tunnelling microscopy, the Aharonov–Bohm effect or the quantum Zeno effect. It counts as two problem sets.

## PHY 302: Statistical Mechanics II

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.08 (with the honours 8.333 option), Harvard Physics 181, Oxford A1/B1, Caltech Ph 127, Dhaka PH 401.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 202, PHY 206.
**Primary:** Reif, *Fundamentals of Statistical and Thermal Physics*.
**Supplements:** Kardar, *Statistical Physics of Particles*; Sethna (free); Tong, *Statistical Physics*.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.333 (Kardar), selected lectures.

1. Ensembles revisited: microcanonical, canonical, grand canonical; fluctuations
2. Ideal quantum gases; the classical limit
3. The degenerate Fermi gas: metals, white dwarfs, the Chandrasekhar limit
4. Bose gases: blackbody radiation, phonons and the Debye model, Bose–Einstein condensation
5. Interacting gases: the virial expansion, van der Waals revisited
6. Phase transitions: the Ising model, mean-field theory
7. **Midterm**
8. Landau theory, critical exponents, universality (with a qualitative account of the renormalisation group)
9. Kinetic theory: the Boltzmann equation, mean free path, transport coefficients (Dhaka PH 102 depth)
10. Brownian motion, the Langevin equation, Einstein's relation
11. Fokker–Planck and master equations; Markov processes (following MIT 8.08)
12. The fluctuation–dissipation theorem, Johnson–Nyquist noise
13. Non-equilibrium topics: stochastic thermodynamics and active matter (MIT 8.08 survey)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## LAB 301: Advanced Laboratory I (Junior Lab)

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.13 (Experimental Physics I), Harvard Physics 191, Caltech Ph 77, Princeton PHY 312, Dhaka PL 301.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 201, LAB 101, CMP 201.
**Primary:** MIT OCW 8.13/8.14 Junior Lab manuals and the original papers each one cites.
**Supplements:** Bevington & Robinson.

As in MIT 8.13, you carry out **four experiments** in modern physics. Two are real home experiments and two analyse **real research data**. Each one ends in a journal-style report. Two of them also get a 20-minute MIT-style oral presentation with questions.

| Weeks | Experiment | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **J1 Planck's constant from LEDs**: turn-on voltage vs wavelength for 4–6 LEDs, wavelengths measured with your own grating spectrometer | Home |
| 4–6 | **J2 Atomic spectroscopy**: build a grating spectrometer; measure mercury and sodium lines (fluorescent tube, street lamp); calibrate; compare with NIST; the Rydberg constant from hydrogen data | Home + NIST data |
| 7 | **Oral presentation 1** | – |
| 8–10 | **J3 Particle physics from CMS open data**: reconstruct the dimuon invariant-mass spectrum; find the J/ψ, Υ and Z; fit the Z mass and width | CERN Open Data |
| 11–13 | **J4 Gravitational waves from LIGO open data**: whitening, band-passing, matched filtering of GW150914; estimate the chirp mass | GWOSC |
| 14 | **Oral presentation 2** | – |
| 15 | **Final**: an unseen data-analysis practical (3 h) | – |

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · reports 45% · orals 15% · pre-lab quizzes 10% · final practical 15%.

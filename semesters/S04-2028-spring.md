## PHY 201: Quantum Physics I

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.04, Harvard Physics 143a, Oxford A3, Princeton PHY 208, Caltech Ph 12b, Dhaka PH 302.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 103, MTH 103, MTH 104.
**Primary:** Griffiths & Schroeter, *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* (3rd ed.), ch. 1–4.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.04 Quantum Physics I (Zwiebach).
**Supplements:** Zwiebach, *Mastering Quantum Mechanics*; French & Taylor; Feynman Vol. III ch. 1–3.

1. The experimental basis: photoelectric effect, Compton scattering, de Broglie waves, the Mach–Zehnder interferometer
2. Wavefunctions, the Born rule, normalisation, expectation values
3. Momentum space, Fourier transforms, the uncertainty principle
4. The Schrödinger equation, stationary states, the infinite well
5. The harmonic oscillator: analytic and ladder-operator methods
6. Free particle, wave packets, group velocity
7. **Midterm**
8. The delta potential, scattering in 1D, transmission and reflection, tunnelling
9. The finite well, bound states, the variational principle (intro)
10. Formalism: Hermitian operators, eigenfunctions, the generalised uncertainty principle
11. 3D: separation in spherical coordinates, angular momentum, spherical harmonics
12. The hydrogen atom
13. Spin ½, the Stern–Gerlach experiment, Pauli matrices
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 202: Thermal & Statistical Physics I

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.044, Oxford A1, Princeton PHY 301, Caltech Ph 12c, Dhaka PH 102.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 101, MTH 102. PHY 201 concurrently, as MIT recommends for 8.044.
**Primary:** Schroeder, *An Introduction to Thermal Physics*, ch. 1–7.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.044 notes and problem sets.
**Supplements:** Blundell & Blundell, *Concepts in Thermal Physics*; Kittel & Kroemer.

1. Probability: random variables, distributions, the central limit theorem (8.044 opens here)
2. Temperature, ideal gases, equipartition, heat and work, the first law
3. Heat capacities, enthalpy; kinetic theory and the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
4. The second law: multiplicity, Einstein solids, entropy
5. Interactions: temperature, pressure and chemical potential from entropy
6. Engines and refrigerators, the Carnot cycle, real cycles (Otto, Diesel, as in DU PH 102)
7. **Midterm**
8. Free energies, Maxwell relations, thermodynamic identities
9. Phase transformations, the Clausius–Clapeyron relation, van der Waals
10. The Boltzmann factor, the partition function, the canonical ensemble
11. Paramagnetism, equipartition revisited, the Maxwell speed distribution
12. Quantum statistics: Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein distributions
13. Blackbody radiation, degenerate Fermi gases, Debye solids (preview)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 203: Special Relativity

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.033, Oxford CP1 (SR part) and B2 (part), Harvard 15a (part), Dhaka PH 301 (part).
**Prerequisites:** PHY 102, MTH 103.
**Primary:** Morin, *Special Relativity for the Enthusiastic Beginner*, then Taylor & Wheeler, *Spacetime Physics*.
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.033 Relativity and 8.20 Introduction to Special Relativity.
**Supplements:** French, *Special Relativity*; Steane, *Relativity Made Relatively Easy*.

1. Galilean relativity, the Michelson–Morley experiment, Einstein's postulates
2. Simultaneity, time dilation, length contraction, clock synchronisation
3. The Lorentz transformation, velocity addition, the relativistic Doppler effect
4. Spacetime diagrams, invariant intervals, causality, paradoxes (twin, pole-in-barn)
5. Four-vectors, proper time, four-velocity and four-momentum
6. Energy and momentum, E = mc², massless particles
7. **Midterm**
8. Collisions, decays, threshold energies, the centre-of-momentum frame
9. Relativistic forces, acceleration, hyperbolic motion, rapidity
10. Relativity and electromagnetism: transformation of E and B, the field tensor (preview of PHY 205)
11. The equivalence principle, accelerated frames, gravitational redshift
12. The Schwarzschild metric (intro), geodesics, light deflection, Shapiro delay, GPS
13. Newtonian cosmology (intro, following MIT 8.033)
14. Revision
15. **Final**

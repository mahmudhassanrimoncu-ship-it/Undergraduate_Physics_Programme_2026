## PHY 305: Nuclear & Particle Physics

**Modelled on:** Oxford B4, MIT 8.276/8.701, Harvard Physics 145, Dhaka PH 304 and PH 406.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 301, PHY 203.
**Primary:** Griffiths, *Introduction to Elementary Particles*, plus Krane, *Introductory Nuclear Physics* (selected).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.701 Introduction to Nuclear and Particle Physics.
**Supplements:** Martin & Shaw; Povh et al.

1. Nuclear properties: size, mass, binding energy, the semi-empirical mass formula
2. The nuclear force, the deuteron, isospin
3. The shell model, magic numbers, spin and parity
4. Radioactive decay: α (Gamow tunnelling), β (Fermi theory), γ
5. Fission, fusion, nucleosynthesis, reactors
6. Detectors and accelerators; how particle physics is measured (links to LAB 301 J3)
7. **Midterm**
8. Relativistic kinematics, cross-sections and decay rates, Fermi's golden rule
9. The particle zoo, the quark model, symmetries and conservation laws
10. Feynman diagrams; Griffiths' toy theory; QED processes
11. QCD: colour, confinement, asymptotic freedom, jets
12. Weak interactions, parity violation, CP violation, neutrino oscillations
13. Electroweak unification, the Higgs mechanism (qualitative), the Standard Model and beyond
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 306: Fluids, Nonlinear Dynamics & Complexity

**Modelled on:** Oxford B1 (Flows, Fluctuations & Complexity), MIT 8.09 (fluids and chaos parts), MIT 8.292J Fluid Physics, Harvard AM 108.
**Prerequisites:** PHY 204, MTH 201, CMP 201.
**Primary:** Acheson, *Elementary Fluid Dynamics* (weeks 1–6), then Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (weeks 8–13).
**Supplements:** Tong, *Fluid Mechanics*; Sethna; Tritton.

1. The continuum hypothesis, the material derivative, mass conservation
2. Euler's equations, Bernoulli, hydrostatics
3. Vorticity, Kelvin's circulation theorem, potential flow
4. Viscosity, the Navier–Stokes equations, Poiseuille and Couette flow
5. The Reynolds number, Stokes flow, boundary layers, drag
6. Waves on water, instabilities, a qualitative account of turbulence (Kolmogorov scaling)
7. **Midterm**
8. 1D flows and bifurcations: saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork
9. 2D flows, the phase plane, limit cycles, the Hopf bifurcation
10. The Lorenz equations, strange attractors, chaos
11. 1D maps, the logistic map, period doubling, Feigenbaum's constant
12. Fractals, Lyapunov exponents, the onset of chaos in experiments
13. Complexity: random walks, diffusion, self-organised criticality, an introduction to networks
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## IND 302: Junior Paper II & Scientific Communication

**Modelled on:** Princeton's second Junior Paper, Harvard Physics 95, Caltech Ph 70, MIT's CI-M requirement.
**Prerequisites:** IND 301.
**Task:** A second, more independent Junior Paper (~6,000 words). It is ideally a **pilot for the Senior Thesis** and includes original work, such as a new numerical study, a new analysis of open data, or an extension of a published calculation. The communication strand runs alongside it.

| Week | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 2 | Proposal |
| 3 | **Talk 1**: a 15-minute talk explaining a recent arXiv paper (in the style of Harvard Physics 95) |
| 6 | **Referee report** on a real published paper |
| 8 | **Talk 2**: your JP II methods and results so far |
| 12 | JP II draft |
| 14 | Final JP II |
| 15 | **Talk 3**: a 20-minute final presentation plus questions |

**Assessment:** proposal 10% · three talks 25% · paper 50% · referee report 15%.

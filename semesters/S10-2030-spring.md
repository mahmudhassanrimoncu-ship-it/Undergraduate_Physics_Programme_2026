## PHY 401: General Relativity & Cosmology

**Modelled on:** Oxford B5, MIT 8.962/8.286/8.224, Harvard Physics 210, Caltech Ph 236, Dhaka PH 308 (part).
**Prerequisites:** PHY 203, PHY 204, MTH 201.
**Primary:** Hartle, *Gravity* (weeks 1–10), then Ryden, *Introduction to Cosmology* (weeks 11–13).
**Lectures:** MIT OCW 8.962 General Relativity (Hughes) and 8.286 The Early Universe (Guth).
**Supplements:** Carroll, *Spacetime and Geometry*; Schutz; Tong, *General Relativity*.

1. The equivalence principle; gravity as geometry
2. Metrics, the line element, geodesics from the variational principle
3. The Schwarzschild geometry: orbits, perihelion precession
4. Light bending, gravitational lensing, the Shapiro delay, GPS
5. Black holes: horizons, Kruskal coordinates, a qualitative account of Hawking radiation
6. Gravitational waves in linearised gravity; LIGO (links to LAB 301 J4)
7. **Midterm**
8. Tensors and differential geometry: covariant derivatives, the Christoffel symbols
9. Curvature: the Riemann, Ricci and Einstein tensors
10. The Einstein field equations; the stress-energy tensor; the Newtonian limit
11. FRW cosmology, the Friedmann equations, cosmic expansion, the Hubble constant
12. Thermal history: nucleosynthesis, recombination, the CMB
13. Inflation, dark matter, dark energy, open problems
14. Revision
15. **Final**

## PHY 402: Quantum Information & Computation

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.370/8.371, Harvard Physics 160, Oxford C2 (part), Caltech Ph 219 (Preskill).
**Prerequisites:** PHY 206. This course draws directly on your CSE background in algorithms and complexity.
**Primary:** Nielsen & Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*.
**Lectures:** MITx 8.370x Quantum Information Science.
**Supplements:** Preskill's Ph 219 notes; Mermin, *Quantum Computer Science*; IBM Qiskit tutorials.

1. Qubits, the Bloch sphere, measurement, multi-qubit states
2. Gates and circuits, universality
3. Entanglement, Bell/CHSH, teleportation, superdense coding
4. Oracle algorithms: Deutsch–Jozsa, Bernstein–Vazirani, Simon
5. The quantum Fourier transform and phase estimation
6. Shor's algorithm; the complexity class BQP
7. **Midterm**
8. Grover's search; amplitude amplification
9. Density matrices, quantum channels, decoherence
10. Quantum error correction: the bit-flip and Shor codes, stabiliser codes
11. Quantum cryptography: BB84, no-cloning
12. Physical implementations: superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photons, NV centres
13. NISQ algorithms: VQE and QAOA; a project run on real IBM Quantum hardware
14. Project write-up
15. **Final**

**Assessment:** problem sets 25% · quizzes 5% · tutorials 10% · midterm 20% · final 30% · **Qiskit project 10%**.

## LAB 401: Advanced Laboratory II, Experiment Design Project

**Modelled on:** MIT 8.14 (an exploratory project developed from proposal and design through to a poster session), Caltech Ph 77, Harvard Physics 191, Dhaka PH 404 and PL 401.
**Prerequisites:** LAB 301.

There is one short guided experiment, followed by a **self-designed experiment** that runs from proposal to poster.

| Weeks | Work |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | **Guided: cosmic-ray muons** with a CosmicWatch-style desktop muon detector (an MIT-designed open-hardware scintillator and SiPM detector, about US$100 in parts). Measure the rate vs angle (cos²θ) and vs elevation |
| 4 | **Project proposal**: physics question, apparatus, expected signal, uncertainty budget, risks |
| 5–6 | Design review and build |
| 7 | **Oral: design review** |
| 8–12 | Data taking, analysis, iteration |
| 13 | Poster and report draft |
| 14 | Final report (journal style) |
| 15 | **Poster session / oral defence** |

**Project ideas:** a Michelson interferometer measuring thermal expansion; a Kater reversible pendulum (g to 10⁻⁴); a chaotic double pendulum vs simulation; an optical-lever torsion balance; acoustic levitation and standing waves; a homemade magnetometer tracking geomagnetic variation; measuring Planck's constant from blackbody radiation; laser speckle statistics.

**Assessment:** notebook 15% · reports 45% (guided report 15%, project report 30%) · orals 15% · proposal and design review 10% · poster/defence 15%.

**Senior Thesis proposal (due at the end of week 13):** 3–4 pages giving the research question, background, method, timeline and risks. Approval is required before THS 401.

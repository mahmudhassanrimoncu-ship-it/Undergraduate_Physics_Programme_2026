# Programme Handbook

*B.S. (Honours) in Physics, self-directed edition. Version 1.0, October 2026.*

This handbook is the "rules of the department". Where a course syllabus says something different, the syllabus wins for that course. Where Rimon and the instructor agree a change, it is recorded in the changelog at the bottom.

---

## 1. Aims

By the end of the programme Rimon should be able to do the following, at the level of a graduating physics major from a top department:

1. **Solve problems.** Set up and solve unseen problems across the core of physics: classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, thermal and statistical physics, waves and optics, and relativity.
2. **Use the mathematics.** Calculus, linear algebra, ODEs and PDEs, complex analysis, Fourier methods, special functions, probability and tensors, used fluently as tools.
3. **Do experiments.** Design an experiment, take data, quantify uncertainty, and write it up as a scientist would.
4. **Compute.** Use numerical and computational methods to solve physics problems that have no closed-form answer. This is where your CSE background is an advantage.
5. **Read research.** Read a research paper in one or two fields, reproduce a result, and present it orally and in writing.
6. **Carry out a research project.** Complete a supervised project and defend it in a viva (Senior Thesis).

The benchmark for "level" is the content of **MIT's Course 8 Focused Option**, **Oxford's MPhys Parts A–C**, **Princeton's departmentals**, **Harvard's Honours concentration**, and **Caltech's Physics Option**. See the [curriculum map](curriculum-map.md).

## 4. Load and the weekly rhythm

You have chosen 18–22 hours per week, which means three courses per semester at about 6–7 hours each.

**Timetable.** Every term has a fixed weekly class timetable, like a university's, in [semesters/timetables/](../semesters/timetables/). Each theory course has 2 × 80-minute lectures and 1 recitation (problem class) per week. CMP courses have 1 lecture and 1 lab. Tutorials and office hours are at the weekend. You can attend a slot live with the instructor at its scheduled time or later that day or week (see [lectures/README.md](../lectures/README.md)). The Orientation Term and S1 timetables are ready. The timetable for each later semester is issued at least **two weeks before** it starts, so it can fit around your CSE timetable for that term.

**Teaching method.** Every lecture, recitation, problem set and tutorial follows the [Teaching Method](teaching-method.md), which is based on cognitive load theory. In short: worked examples come before problems, and support fades as you master each topic. Each lecture opens with a prerequisite warm-up, problem sets move from example pairs to mixed practice, and a 1–9 effort rating after each lecture keeps the load in the productive zone.

A typical week for one **theory course**:

| Activity | Time |
|---|---|
| Lectures (MIT OCW or equivalent video, or tutor-written notes) | ~2–2.5 h |
| Reading the primary textbook, with worked examples | ~1.5 h |
| Problem set | ~2.5–3 h |
| Tutorial with the tutor (written or live, in a thread) | ~0.5 h |

Default weekly deadlines:

- **Monday.** New week's material released: reading list, lecture videos, problem set.
- **Sunday 23:59 (your local time).** Problem set due. Upload a scan or photo, or commit it to `work/` (see §7).
- **Within 48 hours.** Graded and returned with written feedback. The **tutorial** follows: you are asked to explain one or two of your solutions and handle a follow-up ("what if the incline also accelerates?").

A **lab course** replaces most of the problem-set time with experiments, a lab notebook and reports. A **computational course** replaces it with coding assignments and projects.

Expect some weeks to spike to about 25 hours, especially before midterms and finals. That is normal at every university on this list.

## 5. Roles

| Role | What Claude does |
|---|---|
| **Course instructor** | Writes the syllabus, releases each week's material, sets problem sets and exams |
| **Teaching assistant** | Grades problem sets against a rubric, writes solutions *after* the deadline, holds "office hours" whenever you ask |
| **Tutor** | Runs Oxford-style tutorials. Explains anything you are stuck on with hints first, never full solutions to graded work before the deadline |
| **Librarian** | Maintains the [Library](library.md). Recommends the right book or lecture for your exact gap |
| **Editor / advisor** | Gives feedback on writing (lab reports, Junior Papers, thesis). Writes an end-of-semester **report** on strengths, weaknesses and next steps. Adjusts the plan when needed |
| **Examiner** | Sets and grades midterms, finals, annual exams and the viva. Keeps the transcript |

Where to ask: any thread in this project. "Office hours" are unlimited. Use them.

## 6. Prerequisites and placement

- Every course lists its prerequisites in the [curriculum map](curriculum-map.md). To use a course as a prerequisite you must pass it with **C (55%) or better**.
- **Advanced Standing Exams (ASEs).** In the Orientation Term you may sit a 2-hour ASE for **MTH 101, MTH 102, MTH 103, MTH 104** and **CMP 101**. A score of **70% or more** exempts you. The course is recorded as "Credit by Examination" (CR) and carries credits but does not count in your GPA. The freed slot is filled with an **Honours Extra** from the electives list, so the load stays the same. This matters because your CSE degree has probably already covered some of this material.
- If a diagnostic shows a gap (for example, trigonometry or algebra), the Orientation Term gives you bridging work before S1.

## 7. Submitting work

Choose whichever is easier for each piece of work:

1. **Upload in the thread.** Upload photos or a PDF of handwritten work in the course thread.
2. **Commit to the repo.** Put it under `work/<semester>/<course>/<item>/`, for example `work/S01-2027-spring/PHY101/pset03/pset03.pdf`.

Rules:

- **Handwritten is fine and encouraged** for problem sets and exams, which is how they are done at MIT and Oxford. Scans must be legible.
- Typed work can be in Markdown with LaTeX or in proper LaTeX (see [templates/](../templates/)).
- **Box final answers.** For every result, check its **units** and its **limiting cases**. Both are graded.
- Code is submitted as `.py` or `.ipynb` files with a short README.

## 8. Tutorials

Tutorials follow the Oxford model.

- After each problem set is graded, the tutor picks one or two problems and asks you to **explain your reasoning**. This is done in writing or by voice note, in the thread.
- Follow-up questions extend the problem: "what changes if…", "estimate…", "why is this sign negative?".
- **Self-explanation round.** You also talk through the posted solution to the problem you found hardest: why each step is taken, and what would change if the set-up changed (see the [Teaching Method §6](teaching-method.md#6-tutorials)).
- Graded 0–3 per tutorial (0 = absent, 1 = cannot explain own work, 2 = solid, 3 = insightful). The total forms the **tutorial** part of the course grade.

## 9. Honour code

Caltech's Honor Code is: *"No member of the Caltech community shall take unfair advantage of any other member of the Caltech community."* Here the only person you can cheat is yourself. The rules are:

1. **Problem sets.** You may use the textbook, lecture notes and the tutor for **hints**. Don't look at solution manuals, Chegg-style sites, or another AI's answers to the specific problem before you submit. The solved "twin" examples printed in a problem set's Part A are part of the set and may be used. After submitting, use anything you like.
2. **Exams** are **closed book**. The only exception is the formula sheet stated on the exam, usually one handwritten A4 sheet (both sides for a final), as at MIT. Use a simple scientific calculator and no internet. Set a timer and stop when it ends. Write the start and end time on the first page.
3. **Lab data must be real.** If an experiment fails, report that it failed and why. A well-analysed failure scores better than fabricated success.
4. **Writing must be your own.** In the Junior Papers and thesis, quote and cite sources. The tutor can comment on drafts but does not write your text.

A breach gives a zero on that item and a note on the transcript. The honour code only works if it is kept.

## 10. Progression

The full rules are in [assessment-and-grading.md](assessment-and-grading.md). In summary:

- **Pass a course:** ≥ 50% overall **and** ≥ 40% on the final exam.
- **Good standing:** semester GPA ≥ 2.00.
- **Progress to the next year:** pass the year's Annual Examination (≥ 50%) and all of that year's core courses.
- **Probation:** if semester GPA < 2.00, the next semester drops to 2 courses and includes a remedial plan.

## 11. Feedback and review

- **Weekly:** written feedback on every problem set.
- **Mid-semester (Week 8):** a short check-in on what is working and what to change.
- **End of semester (Week 16):** a **Semester Report**, like Oxford's college reports, with grades, strengths, weaknesses, study-habit advice and adjustments for the next semester. Saved in `records/reports/`.
- **Annually:** a curriculum review. Content is added or removed based on your interests, such as an earlier option choice or a new elective.

# Assessment and Grading Regulations

This document sets out how each course, semester, year and the degree as a whole are assessed. It is modelled on MIT and Harvard course grading (problem sets, midterm, final), on Oxford's University Examinations, tutorials and classification, on Princeton's independent work, and on the University of Dhaka's viva voce and 4.0 scale.

---

## 1. Credits

| Item | Credits | Count | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Course (theory, lab, computational or independent work) | 4 | 36 | 144 |
| Annual Examination (written papers plus viva) | 4 | 4 | 16 |
| **Degree total** | | | **160** |

One credit is roughly 25 hours of total work, so a 4-credit course takes about 100–110 hours over a semester.

## 2. Course weightings by course type

### 2a. Theory courses (PHY, MTH, most electives and options)

| Component | Weight | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Problem sets | **25%** | About 10 per semester. The lowest one is dropped. Graded by the rubric in §4 |
| Concept quizzes | **5%** | Short weekly checks (5–10 minutes, multiple choice or short answer) |
| Tutorials | **10%** | Oxford-style oral or written defence of your own solutions, scored 0–3 each |
| Midterm exam | **20%** | Week 7. 90 minutes. Closed book, one-sided A4 handwritten formula sheet |
| Final exam | **40%** | Week 15. 3 hours. Cumulative. Closed book, two-sided A4 handwritten formula sheet |

### 2b. Laboratory courses (LAB 101, LAB 301, LAB 401)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Lab notebook (kept continuously and checked fortnightly) | 15% |
| Lab reports (3–4 per semester, in journal-article format) | 45% |
| Oral presentation(s) of an experiment (MIT 8.13 style) | 15% |
| Pre-lab quizzes and error-analysis exercises | 10% |
| Final practical / data-analysis exam (Week 15) | 15% |

LAB 201 (Electronics) uses the same weights. Its "lab reports" are circuit-build write-ups and its final is a build-and-debug practical.

### 2c. Computational courses (CMP 101, CMP 201)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly coding assignments | 35% |
| Midterm project (Week 7–8) | 20% |
| Final project (a physics simulation or data analysis with a written report) | 30% |
| Code review / oral walkthrough of the final project | 15% |

These courses have no closed-book exam, as with Harvard Phys 20 and Caltech Ph 21/22.

### 2d. Independent work

| Course | Components |
|---|---|
| **IND 301 Junior Paper I** (literature review plus reproduction of a published calculation or result) | Proposal 10% · progress meetings 15% · paper (~5,000 words) 55% · oral presentation 20% |
| **IND 302 Junior Paper II and Scientific Communication** | Proposal 10% · three short talks (Harvard Phys 95 style) 25% · paper (~6,000 words) 50% · referee report on a published paper 15% |
| **THS 401 Senior Thesis I** | Proposal and literature review 25% · progress report 25% · interim results presentation 25% · supervisor assessment of progress 25% |
| **THS 402 Senior Thesis II** | Thesis document (~10,000–15,000 words) 60% · oral defence 25% · poster / public talk 15% |

## 3. Grade scale

Problems are set at MIT, Oxford and Princeton difficulty, so the raw-percentage boundaries are set where a top department's curve usually puts them.

| Letter | Raw % | Grade points |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | 85–100 | 4.0 |
| **A−** | 80–84 | 3.7 |
| **B+** | 75–79 | 3.3 |
| **B** | 70–74 | 3.0 |
| **B−** | 65–69 | 2.7 |
| **C+** | 60–64 | 2.3 |
| **C** | 55–59 | 2.0 |
| **D** | 50–54 | 1.0 |
| **F** | < 50 | 0.0 |
| **CR** | ASE ≥ 70% | credit by examination, not in GPA |

For each letter grade, an equivalent DU grade is also recorded on the transcript (A+ = 4.00, A = 3.75, … under the UGC scale) for reference. The 4.0 scale above is the one that counts.

**Calibration rule.** If an exam turns out harder or easier than intended, the examiner may scale that exam once, up or down, with a written justification, as real departments do. The scaling is recorded in the gradebook.

## 4. How written work is marked

Every problem is marked out of 10:

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Physical set-up: correct principles, diagram, coordinate choice, stated assumptions | 3 |
| Mathematical execution | 3 |
| Correct final result, boxed | 2 |
| Sanity checks: units, dimensions, limiting or special cases, order of magnitude | 1 |
| Clarity: someone else could follow the solution | 1 |

A correct answer with no reasoning earns at most 3/10. A clear, correct method with an arithmetic slip earns 8–9/10. Solutions are released after grading.

**Late work.** −10% per day, up to 3 days, then zero. You get **3 late tokens per semester** (each worth a 24-hour extension, no questions asked), modelled on MIT's late days. Exams cannot be late, but they can be rescheduled before the exam week starts.

## 5. Passing a course

You pass a course if **both** of these hold:

1. Overall weighted mark ≥ **50%** (grade D or better).
2. Final exam mark ≥ **40%**. This is the exam hurdle: you cannot pass on homework alone.

For prerequisites, a course must be passed with **C or better (≥ 55%)** before you start any course that lists it.

**Resit.** If you fail a course, or fail only the final exam hurdle, you may take **one resit** of the final exam, in Week 16 or Week 1 of the next semester. The resit replaces the final exam mark, and the course grade is **capped at C**, as with the capped resits at UK universities.

**Retake.** If you fail after the resit, the course is retaken in full in a later semester and the new grade replaces the old one. The original F stays visible on the transcript, as at MIT and Princeton.

## 6. Semester standing

| Standing | Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Dean's List** | Semester GPA ≥ 3.70 with no grade below B | Noted on the transcript |
| **Good standing** | Semester GPA ≥ 2.00 and every course passed | Continue as planned |
| **Warning** | Semester GPA ≥ 2.00 but a course failed | Resit or retake plan agreed in Week 16 |
| **Probation** | Semester GPA < 2.00 | Next semester drops to 2 courses plus a remedial plan. Two consecutive probations trigger a programme review, and the plan is re-paced |

## 7. Annual Examinations (Oxford-style University Examinations)

The Annual Examination is taken in **Week 16 of each Autumn semester** and covers the whole year's core courses. It tests whether the material has been retained and integrated, not just whether it was learnt course by course.

| Year | Name | Written papers | Viva voce |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prelims** | Paper 1: Mechanics, E&M, Waves (3 h). Paper 2: Mathematical Methods (3 h) | 30 min |
| 2 | **Part A** | Paper A1: Quantum Physics & Relativity (3 h). Paper A2: Thermal Physics, Classical Mechanics & Electrodynamics (3 h). Paper A3: Mathematical & Computational Methods (2 h) | 30 min |
| 3 | **Part B** | Paper B1: Quantum, Atomic & Condensed Matter (3 h). Paper B2: Statistical, Nuclear & Particle, Nonlinear Physics (3 h). Paper B3: General paper: estimation and "Fermi problems", short questions across the whole core so far (2 h) | 45 min |
| 4 | **Finals (Part C)** | Paper C1: General Physics, synoptic across the full four years (3 h). Paper C2: Major Option I (2 h). Paper C3: Major Option II (2 h) | 45 min, combined with the thesis defence |

**Weighting:** written papers 80%, viva 20%. The result is graded on the same scale and counts as a **4-credit item** in the CGPA.

**Progression:** you need **≥ 50%** to progress to the next year. One resit is allowed in Week 1 of the next semester, and the resit grade is capped at C.

**Viva voce:** an oral exam, as in DU's PV 102–402 and Oxford's vivas, covering the year's material and your own submitted work. You are asked to derive, explain and estimate. It is scored by this rubric: correctness 40%, depth of understanding 30%, clarity 15%, handling of unfamiliar follow-ups 15%.

## 8. GPA, CGPA and the transcript

- **Semester GPA (SGPA)** = Σ (grade points × credits) / Σ credits for the courses graded that semester.
- **CGPA** = the same sum over all graded items to date, including Annual Examinations.
- The transcript in [records/transcript.md](../records/transcript.md) records every course: code, title, credits, letter grade, grade points, and any resit or retake. It also records SGPA, CGPA, standing, and Annual Exam results.

## 9. Degree requirements

To be awarded the degree you must have:

1. **160 credits** passed, including all 36 courses or approved substitutes.
2. All **core courses** passed with D or better, and with C or better wherever a course was used as a prerequisite.
3. All **four Annual Examinations** passed.
4. **THS 402 Senior Thesis II** passed with **C or better**.
5. **CGPA ≥ 2.00**.

## 10. Degree classification

Both the UK and the US conventions are given, since the programme borrows from both.

| CGPA | Classification | US equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 3.85 | **First Class Honours with Distinction** | Summa cum laude |
| 3.70–3.84 | **First Class Honours** | Magna cum laude |
| 3.30–3.69 | **Upper Second Class (2:1)** | Cum laude |
| 2.70–3.29 | **Lower Second Class (2:2)** | – |
| 2.00–2.69 | **Pass** | – |

Two extra requirements apply:

- **Honours in the thesis (Princeton/Harvard style).** A First requires THS 402 at **A−** or better. If the thesis is lower, the classification is capped at 2:1.
- **Final-years emphasis (Oxford style).** If the Year 3–4 CGPA is higher than the overall CGPA, the higher figure is used. This rewards a student who finishes strongly.

## 11. Appeals

If you think a mark is wrong, say so in the thread within 7 days. The examiner re-marks the item in full and explains the decision. As at a real university, the re-mark can go up or down.
